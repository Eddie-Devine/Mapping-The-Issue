## Layer intro

> **THE LEFT ROOT: India and the idea of *ahimsa***
> India has the oldest unbroken tradition of vegetarianism in the world. Today about **4 in 10 Indian adults** call themselves vegetarian, and **8 in 10** limit meat in some way [src:pew2021]. It didn't start that way, though.

---

### LEAF ind-1 — It Didn't Start Vegetarian (Vedic Period, c. 1500–500 BCE)
- **Type:** history
- **Text:** The earliest Indian scriptures, the **Vedas**, describe animal sacrifices, and the meat from those rituals was eaten. Over centuries, a new idea grew louder: **ahimsa**, "non-harming." Scholars argue that vegetarianism in India developed gradually *after* the Vedic period, partly as a challenge to animal sacrifice [src:bryant2006].
- **Pixel scene:** A fire altar with smoke; as the visitor moves past it, the scene slowly shifts: the altar fades and a lotus blooms in its place (an "evolution of an idea" transition).
- **Sources:** [src:bryant2006]

### LEAF ind-2 — The Jains: Ahimsa to the Extreme (c. 6th–5th century BCE)
- **Type:** history / figure
- **Text:** **Mahavira**, the 24th great teacher of **Jainism** (traditionally dated to 599 BCE, though many scholars place him later), taught a strict code of non-violence toward humans, animals, plants, and nature. Jains are strict vegetarians, and many avoid **root vegetables** because pulling them up kills the whole plant. The most devoted monks and nuns **sweep the path in front of them** so they don't step on ants [src:natgeo-jainism]. Today **92%** of Indian Jains are vegetarian [src:pew2021].
- **Pixel scene:** ⭐ Signature animation: a white-robed monk sprite walks slowly, sweeping a small broom in front of his feet; tiny pixel ants scurry safely out of the way. Clicking an ant makes it wave.
- **Sources:** [src:natgeo-jainism] [src:pew2021]

### LEAF ind-3 — The Buddha's Middle Way on Meat (c. 5th century BCE)
- **Type:** history / quote
- **Text:** Many people assume all Buddhists are vegetarian. They aren't. In an early text, the **Jīvaka Sutta**, the Buddha says monks may not eat meat "when it is seen, heard, or suspected" that the animal was killed *for them*. Otherwise it may be eaten [src:jivaka-mn55]. Monks begged for food and ate what they were given. To this day, many Theravada (Southeast Asia) and Tibetan monks still eat these "three clean meats" [src:scmp-wu].
- **Pixel scene:** A monk with an alms bowl walks village to village; villagers drop rice into the bowl. A three-panel pixel sign: 👁️ SEEN · 👂 HEARD · 🤔 SUSPECTED, each crossed out.
- **Sources:** [src:jivaka-mn55] [src:scmp-wu]

### LEAF ind-4 — Emperor Ashoka Cuts the Curry (c. 260 BCE)
- **Type:** history
- **Text:** After a bloody war, **Emperor Ashoka** of the Maurya Empire turned to Buddhism and carved his rules into stone across India. His **Rock Edict I** says that where "hundreds of thousands of animals were killed every day to make curry" in the royal kitchen, now only a few were killed, and soon none would be [src:livius-ashoka].
- **Pixel scene:** A tall stone pillar topped with lions; text "chisels" itself onto the stone letter by letter. Pixel peacocks and deer walk away from the palace kitchen, free.
- **Sources:** [src:livius-ashoka]

### LEAF ind-5 — The Yoga Sutras: Non-Harming Comes First
- **Type:** history / quote (Yogi focus)
- **Text:** Patañjali's **Yoga Sutras** (scholars debate the exact date) is one of the oldest guides to yoga. Its first set of moral rules, the *yamas*, begins with **ahimsa**: "non-injury" comes before truthfulness, not stealing, and the rest. The Sutras promise that when non-injury is perfected, hostility around that person disappears (Book II, sutras 30 and 35) [src:yogasutras-johnston]. For many yogis, not eating animals follows from this first rule.
- **Pixel scene:** ⭐ A yogi sprite in lotus pose levitates a few pixels above a mat, bobbing gently. A tiger sprite walks up, sees the yogi, and lies down peacefully next to him (a visual version of "enmity ceases").
- **Sources:** [src:yogasutras-johnston]

### LEAF ind-6 — Food Has a "Mode": The Sattvic Diet
- **Type:** history / quote (Yogi focus, mind)
- **Text:** The **Bhagavad Gita** (Chapter 17) sorts food by the effect it has on the mind. Foods "in the mode of goodness" (*sattva*) "promote life span, and increase virtue, strength, health, happiness, and satisfaction" [src:gita-17-8]. Yogic tradition connects this to a calm, clear mind, the reason yogis built diets around fruit, vegetables, grains, nuts, and milk.
- **Pixel scene:** Three pixel food bowls glow different colors: calm blue-white (sattva: fruit/grains), fiery red (rajas: spicy), dark purple (tamas: stale). Hover each to see a mini mood face.
- **Sources:** [src:gita-17-8]

### LEAF ind-7 — Buddhism Goes Vegetarian in China (c. 520 CE)
- **Type:** history
- **Text:** Buddhism traveled east, and in China it became *more* strict about meat. Around **520 CE**, **Emperor Wu of Liang**, a devout Buddhist, ordered all Buddhist monks and nuns to give up meat. They protested that the Buddha never banned it, but the order stuck, and it's why Chinese Buddhist temples are vegetarian today [src:scmp-wu].
- **Pixel scene:** A pixel Chinese temple; a royal scroll unrolls; monks' bowls switch from 🍖 to 🥬.
- **Sources:** [src:scmp-wu]

## Short branch reaching upward: the tradition today

*(These leaves sit on a thin root that curls up toward the surface, connecting ancient India to the modern world. They can also appear on the trunk timeline.)*

### LEAF ind-8 — Yogananda Brings Yoga West (1920 / 1946)
- **Type:** figure
- **Text:** **Paramahansa Yogananda** came from India to America in 1920 to teach yoga and meditation. His book ***Autobiography of a Yogi*** (1946) became a spiritual bestseller [src:yogananda1946]. He described yogis with total mastery over mind and body, and he followed the vegetarian yogic diet. Many Americans first heard of yoga, meditation, and *ahimsa* through him.
- **Pixel scene:** A steamship sprite sails across a pixel ocean from India to America; a book icon drops and "sells" (counter climbing).
- **Sources:** [src:yogananda1946]

### LEAF ind-9 — Gandhi: "Not Physical, but Moral" (1931)
- **Type:** figure / quote (bridges Positions 1 & 2)
- **Text:** In 1931, **Mahatma Gandhi** told the London Vegetarian Society that "the basis of my vegetarianism is not physical, but moral." He even said he couldn't claim vegetarians were generally healthier than meat-eaters. For him, the reason was ethics, not health [src:gandhi1931]. *(This matters later: it's the exact line between Branch 1 and Branch 2.)*
- **Pixel scene:** A small figure in a white shawl at a podium in a foggy London hall; speech bubble with the quote.
- **Sources:** [src:gandhi1931]

### LEAF ind-10 — Sherpas, the Mountain, and Permission (Himalaya)
- **Type:** culture (links to climbing community)
- **Text:** Before climbing Everest, Sherpa climbers hold a Buddhist **puja** ceremony at base camp. A lama chants, juniper smoke rises, rice is thrown into the air, and the climbers ask the mountain for **permission and safe passage**, and forgiveness for striking it with their axes. Sherpa climbers "will not climb before they are blessed" [src:arnette2013]. Respect for nature comes first. Yet the Tibetan (Vajrayana) Buddhist tradition that Sherpas follow generally allows meat; even its clergy eat "clean" meat [src:scmp-wu]. So in the Himalaya, respect for nature doesn't automatically mean a meatless plate.
- **Pixel scene:** ⭐ Snowy Everest base camp: colorful prayer flags flutter on strings (looped animation), a small stupa with juniper smoke curling up, tiny climbers piling ice axes around it.
- **Sources:** [src:arnette2013] [src:scmp-wu] [src:14peaks2021]

### LEAF ind-11 — The Dalai Lama's Honest Answer (2020)
- **Type:** figure / quote
- **Text:** In a 2020 NPR interview, the **Dalai Lama** said, "We should promote vegetarianism as much as we can," and called heavy meat-eating "very bad for ecology." He also admitted, "we Tibetan also are nonvegetarian." In the cold, high mountains, meat has long been part of life [src:npr-dalai2020]. Ideals and real life don't always match, even for spiritual leaders.
- **Pixel scene:** A radio-microphone icon with a speech bubble; snow falls on a mountain monastery.
- **Sources:** [src:npr-dalai2020]

## Layer takeaway

> **ROOT LESSON:** In India, vegetarianism grew out of an **ethical and spiritual** idea (*ahimsa*, non-harming), not a health trend. The yogis added another idea: food shapes the **mind**. Those two ideas, ethics and mind, grow up through the trunk and become **Branch 2** and **Branch 1**. Even here, though, the tradition was never one-size-fits-all.
