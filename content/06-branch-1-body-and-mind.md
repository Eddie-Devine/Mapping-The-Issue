### "Going meatless can make you healthier, stronger, and calmer."

## POSITION CARD

**CLAIM:** A vegetarian (or mostly plant-based) diet is good for your **body** and your **mind**. You can be strong, healthy, and focused, even at the most extreme level of sport, without meat.

**REASONS**
1. Elite athletes, especially climbers, perform at the top level on plant-based diets.
2. Major nutrition authorities say well-planned vegetarian diets meet all nutrient needs, including for athletes.
3. Plant-heavy diets are linked to better heart health and less exposure to processed meat, which is a known cancer risk.
4. Yogic and Buddhist traditions teach that lighter, plant-based food supports a calm, clear, disciplined mind.
5. It's practical and sustainable for an active, outdoor life.

**WARRANTS (unstated assumptions)**
- If the very best athletes can do it, ordinary people can too.
- Health is mostly a personal choice you can control through food.
- Calm focus and self-control are as important to "strength" as muscle size.
- Protein is protein. The body doesn't care whether it came from a cow or a lentil.

**WHERE THIS POSITION IS WEAK (be fair)**
- One athlete's success is an **anecdote**, not proof.
- The "good for the mind" claim rests mostly on **tradition and personal stories**. Some studies even find *more* depression among meat-avoiders (see Branch 3).
- Vegetarian diets can come with **real risks** if they're poorly planned (B12, bone health, stroke; see Branch 3).

## LEAVES ON THIS BRANCH

### 🧗 LEAF b1-1 — Alex Honnold: The Climber Who Doesn't Need Meat
- **Type:** figure (⭐ hero leaf: the biggest, glowing fruit on the branch)
- **Text:** In **2017**, **Alex Honnold** climbed the 3,000-foot face of **El Capitan** in Yosemite with **no rope**, in **3 hours and 56 minutes** [src:gaffney2026]. The documentary ***Free Solo*** (2018) showed the climb, and also showed Honnold mentioning that he had stopped eating meat a few years earlier [src:freesolo2018] [src:hirsh2024]. If someone whose life depends on his strength doesn't need steak, maybe the rest of us don't either.
- **Pixel scene:** ⭐⭐ The signature animation of the whole site: a tiny climber sprite (generic, not a likeness) scales a sheer pixel granite wall, hand over hand, no rope. A height counter ticks up to 3,000 FT. Wind lines blow past. When he reaches the top, he does a tiny fist pump and a burst of leaves flies out.
- **Sources:** [src:gaffney2026] [src:freesolo2018] [src:hirsh2024]

### 🧗 LEAF b1-2 — "Mostly Vegetarian," Not Perfect
- **Type:** quote
- **Text:** Honnold describes himself as **"mostly vegetarian."** His family "basically never buys meat," but he'll eat it at events or on expeditions rather than waste food. He calls diet **"a spectrum"** and says eating meat once a week is better than every day, and that it "shouldn't be a test of ideological purity" [src:hirsh2024].
- **Pixel scene:** A slider bar labeled CARNIVORE ←→ VEGAN with a little climber icon sitting near the "plants" end, not at the very edge.
- **Sources:** [src:hirsh2024]

### 🧗 LEAF b1-3 — Why He Really Did It (plot twist!)
- **Type:** quote (bridge to Branch 2)
- **Text:** Here's the twist: Honnold didn't quit meat mainly for performance. He said he stopped "**mostly for environmental reasons**," and only "once I stopped, I also kind of pried into the ethics of it" [src:hirsh2024]. He told *Bon Appétit* he makes "a fair amount of my food choices for environmental-type reasons" [src:bilow2015]. Since **2012** he has given **one-third of his income** to his Honnold Foundation, which funds solar energy projects in underserved communities [src:olano2024]. His body is the evidence for Branch 1, but his *motive* sits on Branch 2.
- **Pixel scene:** A vine physically grows from this leaf across the gap and wraps around Branch 2 (this is the first visual hint of the "Graft" in `09-crossroads-1-vs-2.md`). Tiny solar panels glint on a rooftop below.
- **Sources:** [src:hirsh2024] [src:bilow2015] [src:olano2024]

### 🧗 LEAF b1-4 — Van Life & the Dirtbag Tradition
- **Type:** culture (climbing community)
- **Text:** In *Free Solo*, Honnold lives in a **van**, cooking vegetables in a pan and eating straight out of it [src:freesolo2018]. That's part of a long climbing tradition. In the 1950s–70s, climbers at **Camp 4** in Yosemite lived as "dirtbags," spending as little as possible so they could climb all day. Legend Yvon Chouinard reportedly lived on **cat food** to save money [src:nunn2022]. Camp 4 is now on the National Register of Historic Places [src:nunn2022]. Cheap, simple, plant-heavy food fits this lifestyle: no fridge, no grill, no problem.
- **Pixel scene:** A pixel camper van parked under pine trees at night; a camp stove flickers; a spatula flips peppers in a pan. Stars twinkle. Optional: a pixel tent village (Camp 4) with tiny climbers around a fire.
- **Sources:** [src:freesolo2018] [src:nunn2022]

### 🧗 LEAF b1-5 — Steph Davis: "Climbing Better Than I Had Before"
- **Type:** figure (climbing community)
- **Text:** Pro climber and BASE jumper **Steph Davis** went vegan around 2001. She told *National Geographic* she noticed she was "climbing better than I had before" and lists **better athletic performance** as one of veganism's top benefits for her [src:potts2011].
- **Pixel scene:** A second climber sprite (different color) on a desert sandstone tower, waving.
- **Sources:** [src:potts2011]

### 🏙️ LEAF b1-6 — Taipei 101, Live on Netflix (Jan. 2026)
- **Type:** figure / current event
- **Text:** On **January 25, 2026**, Honnold free-soloed **Taipei 101**, a **1,667-foot skyscraper**, live on Netflix, in **1 hour and 31 minutes**. At the top, he said, "It's windy" [src:gaffney2026]. Nine years after El Cap, the "mostly vegetarian" climber is still at the top of his sport.
- **Pixel scene:** A pixel skyscraper with a tiny climber and a "🔴 LIVE" badge blinking in the corner; a wind gust ruffles his hair at the top.
- **Sources:** [src:gaffney2026]

### 🥗 LEAF b1-7 — The Nutrition Experts Agree
- **Type:** evidence
- **Text:** The **Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics**, the largest organization of U.S. nutrition professionals, says "appropriately planned" vegetarian and vegan diets are healthful, nutritionally adequate, and suitable for **all stages of life, including athletes** [src:melina2016]. Plant foods like beans, lentils, soy, nuts, seeds, eggs, and dairy can supply all the protein and essential amino acids the body needs.
- **Pixel scene:** A pixel "nutrition label" building itself out of bean, lentil, tofu, and nut sprites, with a ✔ PROTEIN COMPLETE banner.
- **Sources:** [src:melina2016]

### 🥗 LEAF b1-8 — The Twin Experiment (2023)
- **Type:** evidence
- **Text:** Stanford researchers put **22 pairs of identical twins** on different diets for **8 weeks**: one twin vegan, one omnivore. Same genes, different food. The vegan twins' "bad" **LDL cholesterol** dropped from about 111 to 96 mg/dL, while the meat-eating twins barely changed. Vegans also had about **20% lower fasting insulin** and lost about 4 more pounds [src:landry2023]. Lead researcher Christopher Gardner's takeaway: "What's more important than going strictly vegan is including more plant-based foods into your diet."
- **Pixel scene:** ⭐ Two identical pixel twins side by side. Click to "fast-forward 8 weeks": one twin's heart icon glows healthier green.
- **Sources:** [src:landry2023]

### 🥗 LEAF b1-9 — Hearts and Processed Meat
- **Type:** evidence
- **Text:** In a study following about **48,000 British adults for 18 years**, vegetarians had a **22% lower risk of heart disease** than meat-eaters [src:tong2019] [src:auckland2019]. And in **2015**, the World Health Organization's cancer agency classified **processed meat** (bacon, hot dogs, ham) as **"carcinogenic to humans"** (Group 1) for colorectal cancer, and red meat as **"probably carcinogenic"** (Group 2A) [src:paho-iarc2015].
- **Pixel scene:** A pixel heart with a health bar that fills up; a hot dog sprite with a ⚠️ hazard sign.
- **Sources:** [src:tong2019] [src:auckland2019] [src:paho-iarc2015]

### 💪 LEAF b1-10 — Arnold Changes His Mind
- **Type:** figure (bridges to masculinity debate)
- **Text:** Even **Arnold Schwarzenegger**, the most famous bodybuilder ever, who once ate "10, 15 eggs a day," cut way back on meat. He said, "you really don't have to get your protein from meat — or from animals," and credited the change with lowering his cholesterol. He executive-produced ***The Game Changers*** (2018), a film about plant-based athletes [src:joe-arnold] [src:gamechangers2018].
- **Pixel scene:** A huge muscled sprite flexes, then pulls a pixel broccoli from behind his back like a magic trick.
- **Sources:** [src:joe-arnold] [src:gamechangers2018]

### 🧘 LEAF b1-11 — The Yogi's Mind: Food and Focus
- **Type:** tradition / argument (Yogi & Buddhist focus)
- **Text:** For yogis, food isn't just fuel; it shapes the mind. The **Bhagavad Gita** calls the best foods those that "increase virtue, strength, health, happiness, and satisfaction" [src:gita-17-8], and the **Yoga Sutras** make *ahimsa* (non-harming) the first rule of practice [src:yogasutras-johnston]. **Yogananda** described yogis with total mastery of mind and body [src:yogananda1946]. Picture a yogi holding perfectly still for hours and a climber hanging by his fingertips 3,000 feet up. Both need complete calm and control. Honnold doesn't claim to follow yoga, but both see self-discipline as a kind of strength.
- **Pixel scene:** ⭐ Split screen: left, the yogi sprite levitating in lotus pose; right, the climber hanging motionless from one hand. A shared "FOCUS" meter between them pulses in sync.
- **Sources:** [src:gita-17-8] [src:yogasutras-johnston] [src:yogananda1946]

### 🏔️ LEAF b1-12 — Respect for the Mountain
- **Type:** culture (climbing + Buddhist link)
- **Text:** In ***14 Peaks***, mountaineer Nims Purja describes reaching a summit as his "soul becoming part of the mountain" [src:14peaks2021]. Sherpa climbers hold a Buddhist **puja** before climbing to ask the mountain's permission [src:arnette2013]. Many climbers share a sense that humans are **part of nature, not separate from it**. For some, like Honnold, that respect shows up on the plate.
- **Pixel scene:** Prayer flags strung from this branch's tip, fluttering.
- **Sources:** [src:14peaks2021] [src:arnette2013]

## Branch takeaway

> **BRANCH 1 IN ONE LINE:** You don't need meat to be strong. **Climbers, bodybuilders, twin studies, and yogis** all point to plants fueling both body and mind. The strongest evidence, though, is about **heart health and protein**, not about the mind. And the branch's most famous climber says his real reason is the **planet**.
