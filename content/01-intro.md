## Signpost 1 — THEY SAY…

Three wooden pixel signposts stand in the grass at the base of the tree. Each one pops open a dialog box.

### Sign A — "…it's about the planet."
They say what we eat changes the Earth. Meat and dairy give the world only **18%** of its calories but use **83%** of its farmland [src:poore2018] [src:leap2018]. The 2025 EAT-Lancet Commission report says a shift toward plant-heavy diets could more than halve food-system emissions [src:eatlancet2025]. Others answer that most of what cows eat is grass and leftovers that people can't eat anyway [src:mottet2017].

### Sign B — "…it's about your body."
They say diet is the secret to health and strength. In 2025, **71%** of Americans said they were trying to eat more protein, up from 59% in 2022 [src:ific2025]. In January 2026, the new U.S. Dietary Guidelines flipped the food pyramid and put protein on top [src:fbn2026]. At the same time, nutrition experts say a well-planned vegetarian diet works for everyone from kids to elite athletes [src:melina2016].

### Sign C — "…it's about right and wrong."
They say killing animals is a moral question, and people have been arguing about it for at least 2,500 years. Pythagoras in Greece and the Jains and Buddhists in India all asked whether animals' lives count [src:duncombe2024] [src:natgeo-jainism]. In 2024, more than 200 scientists signed a declaration that even fish and shrimp may have conscious experiences [src:nydeclaration2024]. Others say humans have always eaten meat and that it's natural.

## Signpost 2 — THE MAP (Thesis)

> The vegetarian debate looks like a modern internet fight between gym bros and vegan TikTokers, but its roots go back thousands of years, to human evolution, ancient India, and ancient Greece. This map follows those roots up through time and then out onto **three main branches** where people stand today:
>
> 🌿 **Branch 1: Body & Mind.** Going meatless can make you healthier, stronger, and calmer, as shown by climbers like Alex Honnold and by the yogi tradition.
> 🌍 **Branch 2: Ethics & Earth.** Eating animals harms the animals and the planet, so we should stop, though the movement's loudest voices sometimes hurt their own cause.
> 🔥 **Branch 3: Meat & Might.** Meat is natural, healthy, and part of being human (and, for many, part of being a man). Plant agriculture has costs of its own.
>
> Then the map shows where the branches **grow together** and where they **split apart**, and why.

## Signpost 3 — WHO CARES?

- **Teenagers and athletes**, whose feeds are full of "eat more protein" content and who are deciding what kind of body and identity they want [src:ific2025] [src:velzeboer2024].
- **Parents and families**, who decide what goes on the table every night.
- **Anyone who cares about the planet**, because food systems are a major driver of climate change and land use [src:poore2018] [src:eatlancet2025].
- **Animal welfare advocates and scientists** studying which animals can suffer [src:nydeclaration2024].
- **Religious and spiritual communities** (Hindus, Jains, Buddhists, yogis) for whom food is part of how you live a good life [src:pew2021].
- **Farmers, ranchers, and food companies**, whose livelihoods depend on the answer [src:fbn2026].
- **Policymakers**, who write the dietary guidelines used in school lunches and the military [src:fbn2026].

## Signpost 4 — SO WHAT?

Most people eat three times a day, so food may be the most frequent choice anyone makes. The debate isn't only about nutrients. It's about **identity** (who is "strong," who is "manly," who is "good"), **the planet** (how much land and water we use), and **ethics** (which lives count). Right now the debate is louder than ever. Only about **4% of Americans** call themselves vegetarian [src:gallup2023], but the arguments about meat reach almost everyone: in government guidelines, on podcasts, and on TikTok. If you understand where each side's ideas came from, you can make your own choice instead of having an influencer make it for you.
