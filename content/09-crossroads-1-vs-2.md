## WHERE THEY GROW TOGETHER (Intersection)

1. **Same conclusion: eat fewer animals.** Both branches end up at the same plate: mostly plants [src:melina2016] [src:poore2018].
2. **They borrow each other's evidence.** Branch 2 depends on Branch 1's evidence that humans can be healthy without meat. If you couldn't, the ethical argument would be much harder ("we *need* to kill"). Branch 1 points to the environment as a bonus.
3. **The same people stand on both.** **Alex Honnold** is Branch 1's star athlete, but his reasons are Branch 2's: "mostly for environmental reasons," then ethics [src:hirsh2024]. The **yogi tradition** also sits on both: *ahimsa* is an ethical rule (Branch 2) that yogis believed also calmed the mind (Branch 1) [src:yogasutras-johnston] [src:gita-17-8].
4. **Shared roots.** Both grew from the India and Greece roots, where ethics and self-discipline were tangled together. Seneca's teacher Sextius argued that eating meat made cruelty a habit and harmed your **character**, an argument about both ethics *and* the self [src:duncombe2024].

## WHERE THEY SPLIT (Divergence)

| | 🌿 Branch 1: Body & Mind | 🌍 Branch 2: Ethics & Earth |
|---|---|---|
| **Who benefits?** | **Me**: my health, performance, focus | **Others**: animals, the planet, future generations |
| **Main evidence** | Nutrition studies, athletes, twin trial [src:landry2023] | Environmental data, animal sentience science [src:poore2018] [src:nydeclaration2024] |
| **How strict?** | A spectrum: "mostly vegetarian" is fine [src:hirsh2024] | Often all-or-nothing: if it's wrong, it's wrong |
| **Is a little meat okay?** | Yes, if you're healthy | Not if an animal suffered for it |
| **Typical voice** | Climber, athlete, yogi | Philosopher, scientist, activist |
| **Biggest risk** | Becomes "just another diet trend" | Becomes preachy and pushes people away [src:minson2012] |

## WHY DO THEY SPLIT? (the four diagnostic signposts)

### Signpost 1 — Competing interests?
Partly. Branch 1 is driven by **self-interest** (in a good way: wanting to be healthy and perform). Branch 2 is driven by **other-interest**, sometimes at a personal cost. Gandhi drew this exact line in 1931: he said his vegetarianism was "not physical, but moral," and that a selfish, health-based reason wouldn't keep someone committed [src:gandhi1931].

### Signpost 2 — Focus on different aspects?
**Yes, this is the main cause.** Both branches can agree on every fact and still emphasize different ones. Branch 1 looks **inward** (heart, muscles, mind). Branch 2 looks **outward** (farms, forests, animals).

### Signpost 3 — Different sources of evidence?
Yes. Branch 1 cites **nutrition science and athletes** [src:melina2016] [src:landry2023] [src:potts2011]. Branch 2 cites **environmental science and philosophy** [src:poore2018] [src:singer1975] [src:baker2021].

### Signpost 4 — Same evidence, read differently?
Sometimes. Take **Honnold's diet**. Branch 1 reads it as proof that you can be an elite athlete without meat. Branch 2 reads his *reasons* as proof that people care about the planet. And his "spectrum" approach (some meat sometimes) reads as **reasonable** on Branch 1 but **not enough** to a strict Branch 2 activist [src:hirsh2024].

## Crossroads takeaway

> **THE GRAFT:** Branches 1 and 2 are allies with different motives. One asks *"What's good for me?"* and the other asks *"What's good for everyone else?"* Their answers point to the same plate, which is why people like Alex Honnold can stand on both at once.
