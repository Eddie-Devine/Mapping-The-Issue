## Layer intro

> **THE RIGHT ROOT: Greece and the "Pythagorean diet"**
> Long before the word "vegetarian" existed, Greek and Roman thinkers were already arguing about whether eating animals was right. Their reasons sound surprisingly modern.

---

### LEAF grk-1 — Pythagoras and the Wandering Soul (c. 530 BCE)
- **Type:** figure / history
- **Text:** **Pythagoras**, the same Pythagoras from math class, led a community whose members are among the first known self-described vegetarians in the West [src:avey2014] [src:vegansociety-history]. His followers believed in **transmigration**: when you die, your soul can be reborn in an animal. Eating an animal might mean eating a loved one's soul [src:duncombe2024]. (Historians aren't sure how strict Pythagoras himself was. He may have eaten fish [src:avey2014].)
- **Pixel scene:** ⭐ Pythagoras on stone steps with a little crowd. When the visitor clicks him, a ghostly pixel soul floats out of a sheep and into a person, then back. A tiny right-triangle doodle floats nearby as a wink to math.
- **Sources:** [src:duncombe2024] [src:avey2014] [src:vegansociety-history]

### LEAF grk-2 — Cruelty Is a Habit (Sextius & Seneca, 1st century BCE–62 CE)
- **Type:** quote / argument (early form of Position 2)
- **Text:** The Roman philosopher **Seneca** described two arguments for skipping meat. From the teacher **Sextius**: plants give us all the nutrition we need, so killing animals only serves **pleasure**, and making cruelty a habit damages your **character**. That argument is about what meat-eating does to *you*, not just to the animal [src:duncombe2024].
- **Pixel scene:** A Roman writing at a desk by lamplight; a quill scratches; a letter flies off to "Lucilius."
- **Sources:** [src:duncombe2024]

### LEAF grk-3 — Ovid Gives Pythagoras the Mic (8 CE)
- **Type:** quote
- **Text:** In the poem ***Metamorphoses***, the Roman poet **Ovid** imagines Pythagoras giving a long speech against eating animals. It begins: "Human beings, stop desecrating your bodies with impious foodstuffs" [src:ovid-kline].
- **Pixel scene:** A pixel scroll unrolls; the quote types out in a retro font, one letter at a time, with a typewriter blip sound (if sound is on).
- **Sources:** [src:ovid-kline]

### LEAF grk-4 — Plutarch's Challenge: "Kill It Yourself" (c. 100 CE)
- **Type:** argument
- **Text:** The Greek writer **Plutarch** wrote one of the oldest surviving essays against meat, ***On the Eating of Flesh***. He flips the question: instead of asking why Pythagoras gave up meat, he asks what first made a human put a dead animal in their mouth. He also dares meat-eaters to kill an animal with their own hands and teeth, without knives [src:plutarch-flesh]. *(Interesting twist: modern hunters like Joe Rogan make a version of the same "face where your meat comes from" argument, but they reach the opposite conclusion. See Branch 3.)*
- **Pixel scene:** A Greek sprite stares down a pixel ox; the ox stares back; a "…" bubble. Then both shrug and walk away.
- **Sources:** [src:plutarch-flesh] [src:obrien2015]

### LEAF grk-5 — Porphyry Writes the Book (3rd century CE)
- **Type:** history
- **Text:** The philosopher **Porphyry** wrote ***On Abstinence from Animal Food***, a full book-length defense of ethical vegetarianism and a record of Pythagoras's diet. Its arguments are strikingly similar to modern ones [src:avey2014] [src:duncombe2024].
- **Pixel scene:** A stack of scrolls growing taller as a scribe writes.
- **Sources:** [src:avey2014] [src:duncombe2024]

### LEAF grk-6 — But Most Greeks Ate Meat
- **Type:** context (fairness)
- **Text:** Vegetarians were a **small minority** in the ancient world. Meat was tied to religious festivals and sacrifices to the gods [src:duncombe2024]. Pythagoreans were seen as unusual, a bit like vegans at a barbecue today.
- **Pixel scene:** A festival with a big pixel feast table; a lone Pythagorean sits at the end with a bowl of greens while others cheer with drumsticks.
- **Sources:** [src:duncombe2024]

## Bridge into the trunk (shallow root, ~1700–1847)

### LEAF grk-7 — From "Pythagorean" to "Vegetarian" (1745–1847)
- **Type:** history
- **Text:** Pythagoras's teachings were translated into English in **1745**, and for the next century meatless eaters were called "Pythagoreans." On **September 29, 1847**, the first Vegetarian Society met in **Ramsgate, England**, and the new word **"vegetarian"** replaced the old one [src:avey2014].
- **Pixel scene:** A shop sign reading "PYTHAGOREAN" gets taken down by a pixel worker and replaced with "VEGETARIAN." Confetti.
- **Sources:** [src:avey2014]

## Layer takeaway

> **ROOT LESSON:** The Greeks and Romans argued from the **soul** (Pythagoras), from **character** (Sextius and Seneca), and from **honesty about killing** (Plutarch). These are the ancestors of today's ethical arguments on **Branch 2**, and Plutarch's "face your food" challenge even shows up on **Branch 3**.
