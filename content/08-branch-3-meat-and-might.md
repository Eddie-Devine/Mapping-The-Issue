# 08 — Branch 3: MEAT & MIGHT
### "Meat is natural, healthy, and part of being human. Plant farming has costs too."

**Where it lives on the tree:** The **right branch**. Biome: **autumn hunting grounds / ranch at sunset**: red-orange leaves, a campfire with a grill, a log cabin, rolling pasture with grazing cattle, an elk silhouette on a ridge, a pixel podcast studio built into the branch like a treehouse. Colors: ember orange, deep red, leather brown, dusk purple.

**Assignment requirement covered:** **Position 3**: claim, reasons, warrants, evidence, with sources.

> **For Claude Code (tone):** Present this branch **as fairly and as strongly** as the other two. It is not the "villain" branch. Its best arguments (nutrient risks, grazing land, field deaths, industry-funded science on *both* sides) are real and sourced. Where the branch's popular slogans go beyond the evidence (e.g., "you're less of a man"), label them as **cultural beliefs** and show the research about where they come from.

## POSITION CARD

**CLAIM:** Humans evolved to eat meat. Meat is a dense, complete food that supports strength, health, and energy, and cutting it out carries real risks. Plant agriculture isn't as innocent as vegans claim. For many people, especially men, eating meat (and even hunting it yourself) is part of identity, tradition, and honesty about where food comes from.

**REASONS**
1. Our ancestors ate meat for millions of years; some scientists argue it helped fuel our big brains.
2. Meat-free diets are linked to some health risks (B12, bone fractures, stroke) and, in some studies, worse mood.
3. Most of what livestock eat is grass and by-products humans can't eat; much grazing land can't grow crops.
4. Crop farming also kills animals (field mice, rabbits, habitat loss).
5. Hunting your own meat is more honest and humane than buying factory-farmed meat.
6. Animals, especially fish and shellfish, aren't morally equal to humans; many traditions say humans have a special status.
7. Meat is tied to strength and masculinity; for many men, giving it up feels like weakness.

**WARRANTS (unstated assumptions)**
- What is "natural" (what our ancestors did) is a good guide to what we should do.
- Human needs and traditions come before animal interests.
- Strength and toughness are central to being a man.
- Individual diets won't fix big environmental problems; farming practices matter more.

**WHERE THIS POSITION IS WEAK (be fair)**
- "Meat made us human" is now disputed by newer research (see the Taproot).
- Some key pro-meat studies were **funded by the beef industry**.
- Masculinity claims are **cultural**, not biological, and Branch 1's elite athletes undercut them.
- Its loudest influencers have also been caught faking it (Liver King).

### 💪 LEAF b3-4 — "Real Men Eat Meat": Where That Comes From
- **Type:** cultural belief + research
- **Text:** A common belief on this branch is that a man who doesn't eat meat, or can't stomach killing an animal, is **weak**. Research shows how deep this goes: people rate meat as **masculine** [src:rozin2012]; men who see themselves as very masculine eat more meat and justify it more directly [src:rothgerber2013]; and vegetarian men are often seen as **less masculine** [src:ruby2011] [src:velzeboer2024]. Online, the insult "**soy boy**" mocks men who eat plants [src:hathaway2017]. That's a **cultural** link, not a biological one. But it's powerful: for many men, giving up meat feels like giving up part of who they are.
- **Pixel scene:** A pixel arm-wrestling table: a steak vs. a broccoli. Every time the visitor clicks, the result flips. Neither one wins permanently.
- **Sources:** [src:rozin2012] [src:rothgerber2013] [src:ruby2011] [src:velzeboer2024] [src:hathaway2017]

### 🧬 LEAF b3-5 — "We Evolved to Eat Meat"
- **Type:** argument (callback to the Taproot)
- **Text:** Supporters point to the **fossil record**: butchery marks on bones go back as far as **3.4 million years** [src:wong-dikika], and the classic "expensive-tissue" theory says meat helped grow the human brain [src:aiello1995]. To them, meat isn't a choice; it's our design. *(Note: newer research disputes how much meat drove evolution; see LEAF evo-3 [src:barr2022].)*
- **Pixel scene:** A glowing line connects this leaf down the trunk to the Taproot's evo-1 and evo-2 leaves.
- **Sources:** [src:wong-dikika] [src:aiello1995] [src:barr2022]

### 🩺 LEAF b3-6 — The Health Risks of Going Meatless
- **Type:** evidence
- **Text:** The same 18-year British study that found vegetarians had **less heart disease** also found they had a **20% higher risk of stroke**, about 3 more strokes per 1,000 people over 10 years [src:tong2019] [src:auckland2019]. A follow-up found vegans had **2.3 times** the risk of **hip fractures** compared with meat-eaters [src:tong2020] [src:oxford-fractures2020]. Even pro-vegetarian experts say plant-based diets must be **planned** carefully, and vitamin B12 in particular has to be supplemented or come from fortified foods [src:melina2016].
- **Pixel scene:** A pixel skeleton with a glowing hip joint and a ⚠️; a B12 pill bottle bounces.
- **Sources:** [src:tong2019] [src:auckland2019] [src:tong2020] [src:oxford-fractures2020] [src:melina2016]

### 🧠 LEAF b3-7 — "Low Energy" and Mood
- **Type:** evidence (with an important catch)
- **Text:** A 2020 review of earlier studies found that people who avoid meat reported **more depression and anxiety** than meat-eaters and concluded its results "do not support meat avoidance as a strategy to benefit psychological health" [src:dobersek2021]. **But:** the study was funded in part by the **Beef Checkoff**, the beef industry's marketing program, though the authors said the sponsor had no role in the research [src:nestle2020]. And the studies can't show which comes first: does skipping meat cause low mood, or are people with anxiety more likely to change their diets?
- **Pixel scene:** A pixel energy bar labeled "MOOD"; when clicked, a magnifying glass reveals a tiny cow-shaped "$" tag on the back of the study.
- **Sources:** [src:dobersek2021] [src:nestle2020]

### 🐄 LEAF b3-8 — Cows Eat What We Can't
- **Type:** evidence
- **Text:** A 2017 study by UN food scientists found that **86% of what livestock eat worldwide** is stuff humans **can't eat**: grass, leaves, crop leftovers, and by-products. Producing 1 kg of meat takes, on average, about **3 kg of human-edible grain**, much less than older estimates of 6–20 kg [src:mottet2017]. Supporters argue that grazing animals turn land that can't grow crops into food.
- **Pixel scene:** A pixel cow happily munching grass on a hillside too steep and rocky for a tractor; a tractor sprite tries to drive up, slides back down.
- **Sources:** [src:mottet2017]

### 🌾 LEAF b3-9 — Farming Kills Animals Too
- **Type:** argument + counter-argument
- **Text:** Animal scientist **Steven Davis** argued in 2003 that plowing and harvesting crops kills so many field mice and other small animals that a diet including **grass-fed beef** might cause *fewer* animal deaths than a vegan diet [src:davis2003]. Critics answered that Davis counted deaths **per acre** instead of **per person fed**; when you adjust, vegan diets come out ahead [src:matheny2003]. A 2018 review concluded that it's **"currently unclear"** how many animals die in crop farming [src:fischer2018]. The debate is still open.
- **Pixel scene:** A pixel combine harvester rolls through wheat; tiny mice scatter. Then a calculator sprite redoes the math and a "?" appears.
- **Sources:** [src:davis2003] [src:matheny2003] [src:fischer2018]

### 🐟 LEAF b3-10 — "Animals Don't Have Souls"
- **Type:** cultural / philosophical belief
- **Text:** Many traditions put humans in a special category. In the 1600s, philosopher **René Descartes** described animals as complex **machines** without a thinking mind [src:descartes1637]. Earlier thinkers like Aristotle and Thomas Aquinas taught that only humans have a rational soul, so our duties to animals are limited [src:gruen-sep]. Today, the belief often shows up as "fish and shrimp don't really feel anything." Some scientists still argue that fish **lack the brain structures** needed to *feel* pain, even if they react to injury [src:key2016]. (Branch 2 answers back with LEAF b2-5.)
- **Pixel scene:** A pixel wind-up toy fish with a big key on its back swims in circles. Click it, and the key falls off. The fish looks at you: "?"
- **Sources:** [src:descartes1637] [src:gruen-sep] [src:key2016]

### 🏛️ LEAF b3-11 — The Government Sides with Protein (2026)
- **Type:** evidence / policy
- **Text:** The **2025–2030 U.S. Dietary Guidelines**, released in January 2026, raised recommended protein to **1.2–1.6 g per kg** of body weight and did **not** follow the advisory committee's advice to cut back on red and processed meat. Meat-industry leaders praised the change [src:fbn2026]. For this branch, it's a win: official advice now puts protein at the top of the pyramid.
- **Pixel scene:** The flipped pyramid from the trunk reappears here, glowing.
- **Sources:** [src:fbn2026]

## Branch takeaway

> **BRANCH 3 IN ONE LINE:** The case for meat is about **nature, nutrition, and identity**. Its strongest points are real nutrient risks and the fact that grazing animals can use land crops can't. Its weakest are the claims about manhood, which come from culture, not science.
