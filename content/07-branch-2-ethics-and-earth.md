# 07 — Branch 2: ETHICS & EARTH
### "Eating animals harms the animals and the planet, so we should stop."

**Where it lives on the tree:** The **center branch** (tallest, growing straight up). Biome: **lush green forest canopy** full of life: birds, butterflies, a deer, a cow grazing peacefully, a fish swimming in a little pond on a branch, a globe-shaped fruit. Colors: deep greens, earth browns, ocean blue.

**Thorn offshoot:** A **twisted, thorny side-shoot** grows off this branch: **"The Bramble."** It's where **That Vegan Teacher** lives. It belongs to this branch (she shares its beliefs) but it **grows in the wrong direction**, pushing people away. See LEAF b2-T below.

**Assignment requirement covered:** **Position 2**: claim, reasons, warrants, evidence, with sources.

---

## POSITION CARD

**CLAIM:** Killing animals for food is **morally wrong** when we don't need to, and industrial meat production is **damaging the planet**. People should eat plants instead.

**REASONS**
1. Animals, possibly even fish and shrimp, can feel pain and may have conscious experiences.
2. Meat and dairy use most of the world's farmland but give us a small share of our calories.
3. Livestock are a major source of greenhouse gases and drive deforestation.
4. Humans can be healthy without meat (borrowed from Branch 1), so killing animals is unnecessary.
5. Spiritual traditions (Jain, Buddhist, yogic *ahimsa*, Pythagorean) have taught non-harming for 2,500 years.

**WARRANTS (unstated assumptions)**
- If a being can suffer, its suffering counts morally.
- If you can avoid causing harm, you should.
- The planet's future (and future people) matter as much as our present taste or convenience.
- Individual choices, added up, can change big systems.

**WHERE THIS POSITION IS WEAK (be fair)**
- Not all farmland can grow crops; some is only good for grazing (Branch 3).
- Crop farming kills animals too (field mice, habitat loss); the exact toll is debated (Branch 3).
- Its most aggressive activists can make the whole movement look bad (see The Bramble below).

## LEAVES ON THIS BRANCH

### 🌍 LEAF b2-1 — The Biggest Food Study Ever (2018)
- **Type:** evidence (⭐ hero leaf: a glowing globe-fruit)
- **Text:** An Oxford-led team gathered data from almost **40,000 farms in 119 countries**, covering about 90% of what the world eats. Their findings: meat and dairy give us **18% of our calories** and **37% of our protein**, but use **83% of farmland** and produce **60% of agriculture's greenhouse gases**. Even the lowest-impact meat and dairy harm the environment more than the least sustainable vegetables and grains. Without meat and dairy, global farmland use could shrink by **more than 75%** and the world could still be fed [src:poore2018] [src:leap2018].

- **Sources:** [src:poore2018] [src:leap2018]

### 🌍 LEAF b2-2 — Livestock and the Climate
- **Type:** evidence
- **Text:** The UN's Food and Agriculture Organization estimated that livestock produce about **14.5% of all human-caused greenhouse gas emissions**, including methane from cows' digestion [src:gerber2013].
- **Pixel scene:** A pixel cow with little gray methane clouds puffing out (keep it cute, not gross); the clouds drift up and join a darker cloud over the globe.
- **Sources:** [src:gerber2013]

### 🌍 LEAF b2-3 — Where the Soy Really Goes
- **Type:** evidence (myth-buster)
- **Text:** People sometimes blame tofu-eaters for cutting down rainforests to grow soy. But about **77% of the world's soy is fed to livestock**, mostly chickens and pigs; only about **7%** becomes tofu, soy milk, tempeh, and edamame. In the Brazilian Amazon, the biggest driver of forest loss has been clearing land for **cattle pasture** [src:ritchie-owid-soy].
- **Pixel scene:** A pixel soybean pile splits into streams: a huge stream flows to chicken/pig/cow sprites; a tiny trickle reaches a tofu block.
- **Sources:** [src:ritchie-owid-soy]

### 🌍 LEAF b2-4 — The Planetary Health Diet (2025)
- **Type:** evidence
- **Text:** In **October 2025**, the **EAT-Lancet Commission** reported that shifting the world to a mostly plant-based "planetary health diet" could prevent up to **15 million deaths a year** and more than halve food-system emissions. It isn't strictly vegetarian. It suggests about **15 grams of red meat a day** (roughly one small burger a week) [src:eatlancet2025].
- **Pixel scene:** A pixel dinner plate fills up: half vegetables and fruit, then grains, beans, nuts, and a tiny sliver of meat.
- **Sources:** [src:eatlancet2025]

### 🐟 LEAF b2-5 — Can a Fish Feel Pain?
- **Type:** evidence (answers Branch 3's "animals don't have souls")
- **Text:** In **2003**, scientists found pain-sensing nerve receptors (**nociceptors**) in rainbow trout [src:sneddon2003]. In **2021**, a London School of Economics team reviewed **300+ studies** and concluded that **octopuses, crabs, lobsters, and shrimp** "should be regarded as sentient." The UK added them to its animal welfare law, and a government minister said, "The science is now clear that decapods and cephalopods can feel pain" [src:baker2021]. In **2024**, over 200 scientists signed the **New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness**, saying there's a "realistic possibility" of conscious experience in **all vertebrates (including fish)** and many invertebrates, including **insects** [src:nydeclaration2024].
- **Pixel scene:** ⭐ A pixel fish in a pond on the branch. Click it: a thought bubble appears with "…?" then "!" Optional: a shrimp and octopus wave from the water.
- **Sources:** [src:sneddon2003] [src:baker2021] [src:nydeclaration2024]

### ⚖️ LEAF b2-6 — The Philosopher's Argument (1975)
- **Type:** argument
- **Text:** Philosopher **Peter Singer** argued in ***Animal Liberation*** that what matters morally isn't whether a being can reason or talk but whether it can **suffer**. If it can, its suffering counts. By that logic, causing animals to suffer for food we don't need is wrong [src:singer1975].
- **Pixel scene:** A pixel scale: a human on one side, a pig on the other; it balances at "SUFFERING = SUFFERING."
- **Sources:** [src:singer1975]

### 🕉️ LEAF b2-7 — Ahimsa: The Oldest Version of This Argument
- **Type:** tradition (callback to the roots)
- **Text:** This branch grows straight out of the **India root**. Jains, Buddhists, and yogis have taught **ahimsa**, non-harming, for about 2,500 years [src:natgeo-jainism] [src:yogasutras-johnston]. **Gandhi** made the case plainly: "the basis of my vegetarianism is not physical, but moral" [src:gandhi1931]. And from the **Greece root**, Pythagoras and Plutarch were making ethical arguments around the same time [src:duncombe2024].
- **Pixel scene:** A glowing root-line lights up from this leaf all the way down the trunk to the India and Greece roots (the visitor can click to jump there).
- **Sources:** [src:natgeo-jainism] [src:yogasutras-johnston] [src:gandhi1931] [src:duncombe2024]

### 🦬 LEAF b2-8 — We've Over-Hunted Before
- **Type:** history (callback)
- **Text:** History shows humans can wipe out animals when demand outruns nature. North America's **30–60 million bison** were reduced to **512 plains bison by 1889** [src:wwf-bison]. Supporters of this branch say today's industrial meat system is a much bigger version of the same problem.
- **Sources:** [src:wwf-bison]

### 🧗 LEAF b2-9 — The Climber's Real Reason
- **Type:** figure (graft from Branch 1)
- **Text:** Alex Honnold's main reason for going vegetarian was **environmental** [src:hirsh2024] [src:bilow2015]. He funds solar power through his foundation and worries "that in my lifetime, what I think of as Earth will no longer be the same" [src:olano2024].
- **Pixel scene:** The vine from Branch 1 (b1-3) arrives here and blooms.
- **Sources:** [src:hirsh2024] [src:bilow2015] [src:olano2024]

### 🙏 LEAF b2-10 — The Dalai Lama on Ecology
- **Type:** figure / quote
- **Text:** "We should promote vegetarianism as much as we can," the **Dalai Lama** told NPR in 2020, calling heavy meat-eating "very bad for ecology" [src:npr-dalai2020].
- **Sources:** [src:npr-dalai2020]

### LEAF b2-T1 — Who Is That Vegan Teacher?
- **Type:** figure
- **Text:** **Kadie Karen Diekmeyer**, known online as **"That Vegan Teacher,"** is a Canadian former ESL teacher and animal rights activist. She joined TikTok in 2019 and went viral in **July 2020** with songs about eating animals, building about **1.7 million followers** [src:asarch2021].
- **Sources:** [src:asarch2021]

### LEAF b2-T2 — The Controversies
- **Type:** evidence
- **Text:** Her videos drew heavy criticism for, among other things: saying she wouldn't donate her organs to meat-eaters [src:olle2020]; comparing animal farming to "Nazi death camps" and saying it is "in fact worse" [src:asarch2021]; comparing coming out as LGBT+ to "coming out" as an animal rights activist [src:baska2021]; and a sexual joke in a duet with a **16-year-old** YouTuber [src:asarch2021]. Chef Gordon Ramsay answered her criticism by eating a burger on camera and calling her a "vegan doughnut" [src:askinasi2021]. In **February 2021**, TikTok **banned her account** for "multiple community guideline violations," and new accounts she made were banned too. She replied: "I'm still here and I'm not going to stop" [src:asarch2021].
- **Pixel scene:** A TikTok-style phone frame (generic, no logo) shows a red "ACCOUNT BANNED" stamp. Thorns grow around it.
- **Sources:** [src:asarch2021] [src:olle2020] [src:baska2021] [src:askinasi2021]

### LEAF b2-T3 — Why Extremists Backfire: The Research
- **Type:** evidence (supports the claim that she hurts the cause)
- **Text:** Psychologists have studied exactly this. In one set of studies, people saw typical activists as "**militant**" and "**eccentric**," and those stereotypes made people **less willing to adopt the very behaviors the activists promoted** [src:bashir2013]. Another study asked meat-eaters to describe vegetarians, and nearly half used negative words like "**self-righteous**," "**preachy**," and "**judgmental**." The more people expected vegetarians to judge them, the more they disliked vegetarians [src:minson2012]. Researchers have also found that vegans face bias comparable to some other minority groups [src:macinnis2017].
- **Pixel scene:** A pixel "approval meter" next to the branch. Each time the megaphone blasts, the meter drops and an NPC walks away.
- **Sources:** [src:bashir2013] [src:minson2012] [src:macinnis2017]

### LEAF b2-T4 — The Bramble vs. the Climber
- **Type:** analysis
- **Text:** Compare two people who share the same basic belief that eating less meat is better. **That Vegan Teacher** called meat-eaters despicable [src:olle2020] and compared farms to death camps, and she was banned. **Alex Honnold** calls diet "a spectrum" and says it "shouldn't be a test of ideological purity" [src:hirsh2024]. Research on "do-gooder derogation" suggests why the second message travels further: people push back hardest when they feel **morally judged** [src:minson2012]. Radicals don't just fail to persuade. They give opponents a cartoon villain to point at.
- **Pixel scene:** Side-by-side: the megaphone figure (people walking away) vs. the climber sprite (people walking toward him, curious).
- **Sources:** [src:olle2020] [src:hirsh2024] [src:minson2012] [src:asarch2021]

## Branch takeaway

> **BRANCH 2 IN ONE LINE:** The case against meat is mostly about **others**: the animals that may suffer and the planet we share. It has the oldest roots (*ahimsa*, Pythagoras) and some of the strongest modern data (Poore & Nemecek). Its biggest threat may be its own loudest voices.
