## 🟢 GREEN VINE — The Vegetarian Movement Is Born

### LEAF trk-g1 — The First Ethical Vegans (1806)
- **Text:** In **1806**, Dr. William Lambe and the poet Percy Bysshe Shelley were among the first Europeans to publicly object to eating **eggs and dairy** on ethical grounds [src:vegansociety-history].
- **Sources:** [src:vegansociety-history]

### LEAF trk-g2 — Vegetarian Churches (1809–1817)
- **Text:** The Bible Christian Church, founded in England in **1809**, asked members to avoid meat. In **1817**, William and Susanna Metcalfe brought the idea to **Philadelphia** and started America's first vegetarian church [src:avey2014].
- **Sources:** [src:avey2014]

### LEAF trk-g3 — A New Word: "Vegetarian" (1847–1850)
- **Text:** The Vegetarian Society formed in Ramsgate, England, on **September 29, 1847**. In **1850**, Sylvester Graham (yes, the graham cracker guy) helped found the **American Vegetarian Society** in New York [src:avey2014].
- **Pixel scene:** A tiny pixel graham cracker pops out when the leaf opens.
- **Sources:** [src:avey2014]

### LEAF trk-g4 — The Jungle (1906)
- **Text:** Upton Sinclair's novel ***The Jungle*** exposed filthy, dangerous conditions in Chicago's meatpacking plants. The public outrage helped pass the **Pure Food and Drug Act** and led to the FDA [src:avey2014] [src:sinclair1906].
- **Sources:** [src:avey2014] [src:sinclair1906]

### LEAF trk-g5 — Cornflakes and Fake Meat (early 1900s)
- **Text:** **John Harvey Kellogg**, inventor of cornflakes, promoted vegetarianism for health into the 1940s and created **protose**, an early meat substitute [src:avey2014].
- **Sources:** [src:avey2014]

### LEAF trk-g6 — "Vegan" Is Born (1944)
- **Text:** In **November 1944**, Donald Watson and five other non-dairy vegetarians founded **The Vegan Society** in England. Watson made the word "vegan" from the first three and last two letters of "vegetarian," "the beginning and end of vegetarian" [src:vegansociety-history].
- **Sources:** [src:vegansociety-history]

### LEAF trk-g7 — Diet for a Small Planet (1971)
- **Text:** Frances Moore Lappé's bestseller ***Diet for a Small Planet*** argued that feeding grain to animals wastes food and showed how to get enough protein from plants. It's one of the first books to tie diet to **the environment** [src:lappe1971] [src:avey2014].
- **Sources:** [src:lappe1971] [src:avey2014]

### LEAF trk-g8 — Animal Liberation (1975)
- **Text:** Philosopher **Peter Singer** published ***Animal Liberation***, arguing that animals' suffering counts morally and that factory farming causes enormous suffering. It launched the modern animal rights movement [src:singer1975].
- **Sources:** [src:singer1975]

### LEAF trk-g9 — The Science Gets Big (2013–2018)
- **Text:** A **2013** UN Food and Agriculture Organization report estimated that livestock produce about **14.5%** of human-caused greenhouse gas emissions [src:gerber2013]. In **2018**, the largest study of its kind (nearly **40,000 farms in 119 countries**) concluded that avoiding meat and dairy is "the single biggest way" to reduce your environmental impact [src:poore2018] [src:leap2018].
- **Sources:** [src:gerber2013] [src:poore2018] [src:leap2018]

### LEAF trk-g10 — Plant-Based Goes Mainstream (2019)
- **Text:** The documentary ***The Game Changers***, executive-produced by **Arnold Schwarzenegger**, hit Netflix and pitched plant-based eating to athletes [src:gamechangers2018] [src:joe-arnold]. That same year, **Beyond Meat's** stock jumped **163%** on its first day of trading, the biggest IPO pop since the 2008 financial crisis [src:bloomberg-beyond2019].
- **Pixel scene:** A pixel stock chart rockets upward with a plant-burger riding it like a surfboard.
- **Sources:** [src:gamechangers2018] [src:joe-arnold] [src:bloomberg-beyond2019]

### LEAF trk-g11 — Still a Small Club (2023)
- **Text:** For all the buzz, Gallup found that only **4%** of Americans identify as vegetarian and **1%** as vegan. That number has barely moved since 1999 [src:gallup2023].
- **Sources:** [src:gallup2023]

## 🟡 GOLD VINE — The Body Industry: From Strongmen to Influencers

### LEAF trk-y1 — The First Fitness Influencer (1890s)
- **Text:** **Eugen Sandow** became famous in the 1890s for his muscles, posing like Greek statues on stage. He was filmed by Thomas Edison's studio in 1894, launched a fitness magazine in 1898, and sold dumbbells, supplements, and mail-order courses with **before-and-after photos**. Historians call him the **original fitness influencer** [src:klein2026-sandow]. In **1901** he ran the first major bodybuilding contest at London's Royal Albert Hall [src:smithsonian-bodybuilders].
- **Pixel scene:** ⭐ A pixel strongman in a leopard-print singlet flexes on a stage; old-timey camera flash; "BEFORE / AFTER" cards flip.
- **Sources:** [src:klein2026-sandow] [src:smithsonian-bodybuilders]

### LEAF trk-y2 — Physical Culture (1899)
- **Text:** **Bernarr Macfadden** founded ***Physical Culture*** magazine in **1899**, spreading the idea that diet and exercise could transform your body and life [src:smithsonian-bodybuilders].
- **Sources:** [src:smithsonian-bodybuilders]

### LEAF trk-y3 — Fitness on TV (1951–1985)
- **Text:** **Jack LaLanne's** exercise TV show ran from **1951 to 1985**, bringing workouts into American living rooms [src:smithsonian-bodybuilders].
- **Sources:** [src:smithsonian-bodybuilders]

### LEAF trk-y4 — Arnold Era (1970s–1990)
- **Text:** Bodybuilder **Arnold Schwarzenegger** became a movie star and, in **1990**, chairman of the President's Council on Physical Fitness and Sports [src:smithsonian-bodybuilders]. He was famous for eating "10, 15 eggs a day." Decades later, he said "you really don't have to get your protein from meat" and cut back on meat for his health [src:joe-arnold].
- **Pixel scene:** A muscular sprite flexes next to a giant pixel egg carton; later the carton swaps to a salad bowl.
- **Sources:** [src:smithsonian-bodybuilders] [src:joe-arnold]

### LEAF trk-y5 — Ancestral Influencers and the Liver King (2022)
- **Text:** Social media brought a new wave of "eat like a caveman" influencers. The **Liver King** (Brian Johnson) built a huge following eating raw liver and bull testicles and claimed his huge muscles came from his "ancestral" diet alone. In **December 2022** he admitted, "I lied… Yes, I've done steroids" [src:hearing2022].
- **Pixel scene:** A shirtless pixel king with a crown chomps a red liver; a "BUSTED" stamp slams down with a syringe icon.
- **Sources:** [src:hearing2022]

### LEAF trk-y6 — The Protein Craze (2022–2025)
- **Text:** Interest in diet and health is higher than ever. In 2025, **71%** of Americans said they were actively trying to eat protein, up from **59%** in 2022, even though many weren't sure how much they actually need [src:ific2025].
- **Pixel scene:** A pixel phone screen scrolls an endless feed of protein shakes, chicken breasts, and flexing arms.
- **Sources:** [src:ific2025]

### LEAF trk-y7 — The Government Flips the Pyramid (Jan. 2026)
- **Text:** In **January 2026**, the U.S. government released new Dietary Guidelines with an **upside-down food pyramid**: protein, healthy fats, vegetables, and fruit on top; whole grains at the narrow bottom. They raised the suggested protein to **1.2–1.6 grams per kilogram** of body weight (up from 0.8) and did **not** adopt the expert committee's advice to cut red and processed meat. The meat industry praised it [src:fbn2026].
- **Pixel scene:** ⭐ A pixel food pyramid literally flips upside down with a "whoosh" and lands balanced on its point, wobbling.
- **Sources:** [src:fbn2026]

## 🩷 PINK VINE — Thin for Her: Women and Diet Pressure

> **Framing note for Claude Code:** Present this vine factually and kindly. It's about how *advertisers and culture* pushed women toward thinness, not about judging anyone's body. Don't show calorie numbers, weights, or "goal body" imagery.

### LEAF trk-p1 — "Reach for a Lucky Instead of a Sweet" (1928)
- **Text:** In **1928**, Lucky Strike cigarettes told women to "**Reach for a Lucky instead of a sweet**," selling cigarettes as a way to stay slim. The candy industry was so angry it threatened to sue [src:stanford-lucky].
- **Pixel scene:** A 1920s flapper sprite next to a candy jar; a giant "NO" sign blocks the candy. (Show no cigarette brand logos; use a generic pack.)
- **Sources:** [src:stanford-lucky]

### LEAF trk-p2 — Virginia Slims: "You've Come a Long Way, Baby" (1968)
- **Text:** In **1968**, Philip Morris launched **Virginia Slims**, a cigarette made only for women. Its ads borrowed the language of women's liberation ("You've come a long way, baby"), while the long, thin cigarette and the name "**Slims**" connected smoking to a **slim figure**, the body type pushed by fashion magazines and models [src:stanford-virginia-slims].
- **Pixel scene:** A 1960s pixel billboard with a generic stylish figure and the slogan; the cigarette is drawn extra-long and thin to show the "slim" message. (Don't reproduce the real ads; link to the Stanford archive instead.)
- **Sources:** [src:stanford-virginia-slims]

### LEAF trk-p3 — Women Lead the Meatless Movement
- **Text:** Today, women are **three times** as likely as men to call themselves vegetarian (**6% vs. 2%**) [src:gallup2023]. Some reasons are ethical and health-related. But researchers have also found that teen and young-adult vegetarians eat more fruits and vegetables *and* report more **disordered-eating behaviors** than non-vegetarians. Doctors are told to ask *why* a young person is going vegetarian [src:robinsonobrien2009].
- **Sources:** [src:gallup2023] [src:robinsonobrien2009]
- **Note for Claude Code:** Since this leaf mentions disordered eating, add one small line under it: *"If food or body image is weighing on you, talking to a trusted adult or doctor is a strong move."* No hotline numbers needed unless the student adds a verified one.

### LEAF trk-p4 — The Sexual Politics of Meat (1990)
- **Text:** In 1990, writer **Carol J. Adams** argued in ***The Sexual Politics of Meat*** that Western culture links **meat with men and power**, and vegetables and "lighter" food with women [src:adams1990]. Her book set up the next vine.
- **Sources:** [src:adams1990]

## 🔴 RED VINE — Meat for Him: Masculinity

### LEAF trk-r1 — "Is Meat Male?" (2012)
- **Text:** Researchers asked people in the U.S. and Britain about foods and found that people rate meat as **more masculine** than vegetables. They also found meat linked to masculine words in **23 languages** that give nouns a gender. One researcher summed up how many men see red meat: "a strong, traditional, macho, bicep-flexing, All-American food" [src:rozin2012] [src:eurekalert-rozin2012].
- **Pixel scene:** A pixel steak flexes a tiny bicep. A sad pixel tofu block sits in the corner wearing a party hat.
- **Sources:** [src:rozin2012] [src:eurekalert-rozin2012]

### LEAF trk-r2 — "Real Men Don't Eat (Vegetable) Quiche" (2013)
- **Text:** A 2013 study found that men tend to defend meat-eating **directly**: they endorse pro-meat attitudes, deny that animals suffer much, see animals as lower than humans, and cite religion or health. Women more often defend it **indirectly**, by not thinking about where meat comes from. The men's strategies were tied to **masculinity** and to eating **more meat**. In short, many men eat meat partly because it makes them feel like real men [src:rothgerber2013].
- **Sources:** [src:rothgerber2013]

### LEAF trk-r3 — The "Soy Boy" Insult (2017)
- **Text:** Around **2017**, "**soy boy**" spread online as an insult for men seen as weak or feminine. It's based on the claim that soy raises estrogen in men, which reporters noted isn't clearly backed by clinical evidence [src:hathaway2017].
- **Pixel scene:** A speech bubble with "SOY BOY!" that cracks and crumbles when clicked, revealing a "[citation needed]" tag.
- **Sources:** [src:hathaway2017]

### LEAF trk-r4 — What the Research Adds Up To (2024)
- **Text:** A 2024 review of studies on **masculinity and meat** found that men who strongly follow traditional masculine ideals eat more meat and resist cutting back, and that vegetarian and vegan men are often seen as **less masculine or weak**. It also found that many vegetarian men "reframe" their diet as masculine by talking about **strength, discipline, and courage** [src:velzeboer2024].
- **Sources:** [src:velzeboer2024]

## Trunk takeaway (banner right before the split)

> **WHY NOW?** Four forces are twisting together at once: a 200-year-old **vegetarian movement** armed with new climate science; a **fitness industry** that turned bodies into brands; decades of **diet pressure on women**; and a culture that ties **meat to manhood**. Add social media and a government that just flipped the food pyramid, and you get today's debate. **The trunk now splits three ways.**
